What it does

Everything Meta's library should have had

Open the library the way you always do. The difference is what you see.

🗂

Installed and owned, together

Games you own but haven't installed sit next to the ones you have. One tap switches between all, installed and not installed.

🕘

Sorted by last played

The game you were just in is first. Drag a tile where you want it and it stays there.

📁

Favorites, folders, genres, search

Group your games your way, filter by genre, find anything by name. Hide what you never use.

🏷

Marks that work themselves out

New in your library, never launched, demo, barely played. Add your own: completed, didn't like.

💾

Forgotten games, with sizes

See what you haven't played in 30 days (you choose), biggest first, to know what to uninstall to free up space.

▶

A bar for the running game

Open the menu while playing and get "back to the game" and "quit the game" right there, instead of a blocked panel.

🌗

Mixed reality button

One button in the bottom row switches your home between mixed reality and Meta's virtual environment, like a double tap on the headset.

🖼

Looks like it belongs

Neutral greys that match Meta's shell, with the cover of your most played game softly in the background.

🌍

Backup & languages

Save and load your settings as a file. English, Russian, German, Spanish and French.

Install

Three steps, no computer needed

You need developer mode once, because Quest Shelf is installed from outside Meta's store.

Turn on developer mode

In the Meta Horizon phone app: Devices → your headset → Developer mode. Needs a free developer account.

Install the APK

Download it from the latest release and install it with SideQuest, or run adb install QuestShelf-1.0.1.apk.

Follow the short guide

Open Quest Shelf from Unknown sources. It walks you to two system screens, Accessibility and Usage access, where you switch it on. Done.

To go back to Meta's library at any time, use the pause button inside the app, switch the service off on the same Accessibility screen, or uninstall the app. Nothing in the system is changed for good.

Privacy & permissions

No account. No analytics. Nothing sold.

Everything the app knows about you stays on your headset.

What it sends

  • Only to load game names, genres and covers: the package name, title or store id of a game, to OculusDB (and a spare source on GitHub).
  • Nothing about you, your account, your play time or your settings.
  • One switch turns all network use off.

Why it asks for permissions

  • Accessibility service: the only way to start by itself and notice that Meta's library opened. It watches which windows are open, and nothing else.
  • Usage access: your own play statistics, "last played" and app sizes.
  • The source is open, so you can check all of this.

Questions

Good to know

Is it safe? Will it break my headset?

It's a normal Android app that doesn't touch the system. It can be switched off or uninstalled at any time and Meta's library comes right back. The code is open (MIT).

Which headsets does it work on?

It was built and tested on Quest 3 with Horizon OS v209. It may work on other Quest headsets, but that hasn't been tested.

Why does it need an accessibility service?

Android only lets an app start by itself after the headset boots, or react to the Meta button, through an accessibility service. Quest Shelf uses it to notice that Meta's library window opened and to open itself instead. It doesn't read what other apps show, and it doesn't tap or type for you.

Can a Meta update break it?

Possibly. It relies on how Horizon OS behaves today, partly on things Meta doesn't document. If it ever misfires over and over, it pauses itself so that Meta's library stays reachable, and an update will follow.

Is it free?

Yes, free and open source under the MIT license. No ads, no account.

Is it from Meta?

No. Quest Shelf is an unofficial tool and isn't affiliated with Meta. "Meta" and "Quest" belong to their owners.
